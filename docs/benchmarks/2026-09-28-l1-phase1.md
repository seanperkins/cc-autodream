## Notes

- `abstain_excess` is measured against Haiku's historical outcome for the same session (the Phase 1 stand-in for the reference), so a Haiku candidate scores 0 by construction.
- Rates show a 95% Wilson interval and the denominator. Differences smaller than the interval are within noise.
- Evidence is graded in tiers: verbatim quote, else anchored (its quoted commands and identifiers appear in the transcript), else unverifiable (nothing checkable) or ungrounded. Only ungrounded counts as hallucinated. Production L1 mostly paraphrases, so verbatim is informational.
- Cost is the CLI's API-equivalent list cost, shown for information: runs use the subscription.

## Acceptance (2026-09-28)

- 60 frozen sessions (seed 7), one rep per candidate, concurrency 2, the `claude` CLI on the subscription. 0 errors on every run and the served model was verified on every row.
- **Sonnet 5.5 at `low` fails the gate on `abstain_excess`: 53 of 53 (100%).** It returned no findings at all and finished in about 7 seconds per session.
- **Sonnet 5.5 at `medium` fails the gate on `abstain_excess`: 42 of 53 (79%).** The 2026-09-28 production experiment measured 18 of 20 (90%).
- **Haiku 4.5 passes the gate.** Its `abstain_excess` is 4 of 53 (7.5%) against its own earlier outputs, which is run-to-run noise for the incumbent: the 10% ceiling has little headroom above it.
- Spot-check of three failing Sonnet `medium` rows: in one, fresh Haiku also abstained; in the other two, Sonnet stopped after 4 turns and its own summary says the transcript was only partly reviewed or describes a different part of the session than Haiku did. The abstentions are real model behavior, not a grader artifact.
- Sonnet reads roughly a third of the tokens Haiku does (about 17K against 52K input at `medium`), which is why it is faster and cheaper per call and why it abstains.
- Phase 1 has no quality ranking beyond the gate. Phase 2 adds agreement with a reference and a judge.
